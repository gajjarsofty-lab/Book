## 4. Verifiability of 6A and OEKO-TEX

- **6A**: industry shorthand for raw-silk grade, voluntary, not regulated, and it grades raw fiber, not the finished case. Treat every 6A / 6A+ claim as unverifiable. Score impact: zero.
- **OEKO-TEX**: verifiable. Find the certificate number on the label or listing, enter it at oeko-tex.com Label Check, and confirm it is valid and covers bedding/pillowcases. Certificates run 12 months. It confirms chemical safety, not silk content or grade.
- None of the certificate numbers for these products could be checked here.

## 5. Washing: All Free & Clear

Findings:
- **All Free & Clear "The Original" liquid**: retailer ingredient lists show water, C12-15 alcohol ethoxylate, sodium laureth sulfate, sodium carbonate, soap/fatty-acid salts, sodium polyacrylate, benzisothiazolinone. **No enzymes listed.**
- **All Free & Clear Advanced Oxi**: contains **protease**, amylase, mannanase. SDS pH 10.6–11.4. Avoid on silk.
- **All with Stainlifters Free & Clear**: marketed as a multi-enzyme formula. SDS pH 11.0. Avoid on silk.
- All "all" detergent SDS values found are alkaline: pH 10.6–12.3. The Original's own pH was not found.

Why it matters:
- Protease digests protein. Silk is protein (fibroin). Lab data show strength loss at 0.1% protease, 40 °C.
- Silk tolerates acid better than alkali. Repeated alkaline washing dulls and weakens silk. The rate at cold-wash dilution is not quantified in the sources found.

Recommendation:
- Check your bottle. If it says Stainlifters or Oxi → do not use it on silk.
- If it is The Original: no protease, but alkaline. Acceptable short-term for cheap, replaceable cases. Not ideal.
- Cheapest enzyme-free switch: **Woolite Delicates, 16 oz, about $5** (HEB $4.97). Retailers say no enzymes, bleach, or phosphates. pH not verified. One bottle covers many pillowcase loads, so switching costs less than $10 a year.

Break-even: premium matches budget cost per year only if it lasts:
- C1: 3.6× as long as A (A every 6 mo → C1 must last ~22 mo).
- C2: 5.2× as long as A (A every 6 mo → C2 must last ~31 mo).

What limits lifespan for you:
- Yellowing comes from sweat, skin oil, and skincare residue building up in the fiber. On pure white silk, this shows early.
- Higher momme adds abrasion and tear resistance. It gives no stain resistance.
- If you replace on stains, the limit is the same for 16 and 22 momme. A premium case still gets replaced on schedule, so its extra durability is wasted.
- This is reasoning from mechanism, not measured product data.

6. **Is $60–90 for two worthwhile for you? No.**
   - Your replacement trigger is staining, and momme does not change it.
   - THXSILK must last 3.6× and Mulberry Park 5.2× as long as a $26 pair just to break even.
   - The only real gain from paying more is authenticity confidence. ZIMASILK at about $52 for two buys most of that confidence for far less than the 22-momme benchmarks.

7. **Most economical washing and replacement strategy**:
   - Buy the 2-pack, or buy 2 extra later. Rotating cases spreads wear.
   - Wash weekly: cold water, mesh bag, delicate cycle or hand wash, enzyme-free detergent (Woolite Delicates), no softener, no bleach, air dry, no dryer.
   - Optional: add a little white vinegar to the rinse. Seller care sheets recommend it. The effect is unverified.
   - Let styling cream and moisturizer absorb before lying down. Less transfer means slower yellowing.
   - Replace when stains bother you. Do not pay a premium to delay that.

## 8. Verify on arrival (5 minutes)

1. Burn test one loose thread from an inside seam. Silk: slow burn, singed-hair smell, crushable ash. Polyester: melts into a hard bead.
2. Check the care label: 100% mulberry silk, both panels.
3. Measure: 20 × 30 in.
4. Zipper: hidden, stitched evenly, opens without snagging fabric.
5. OEKO-TEX number → oeko-tex.com Label Check.
6. Return within the window if any check fails.
